# Wikimedia Project Catalog

Wikimedia is a movement, not one website. The Wikimedia Foundation supports the servers and the brand, but volunteers write the content and make the rules. Each project below is a separate wiki with its own policies, help pages, and community.

When a link is in English, look for a language selector or a translate link. Rules can also differ between language editions of the same project.

## Content projects

### Wikipedia

- **What it is:** A free encyclopedia with separate editions for many languages. Articles must be written from a neutral point of view and use reliable sources.
- **Good for:** People who like reading, checking facts, writing clearly, and improving articles about topics they know.
- **Safe first task:** Fix a typo, add a missing link, or add one reliable source to a short article in your language.
- **Start here:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Contributing_to_Wikipedia
- **Watch out for:** Notability rules, copyright, conflict-of-interest editing, and different rules on each language edition.

### Wikimedia Commons

- **What it is:** A shared library of free photos, diagrams, audio, and video used by Wikipedia and other projects. Files must be free to reuse.
- **Good for:** Photographers, illustrators, audio recorders, and people who like organizing media.
- **Safe first task:** Upload three photos you took yourself, with a clear title, date, description, categories, and the correct license.
- **Start here:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:First_steps
- **Watch out for:** Only upload your own work or clearly licensed files. Check project scope. Get consent where needed for photos of identifiable people.

### Wikidata

- **What it is:** A shared database of facts. It stores items, statements, labels, and links that other projects can reuse.
- **Good for:** People who like structured data, lists, sources, and connecting information across languages.
- **Safe first task:** Add one missing fact with a source to an item about a topic you know, and write an edit summary.
- **Start here:** https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Introduction
- **Watch out for:** Notability, sourcing, and the risk of damaging many pages with a single change. Do not start with batch edits.

### Wikisource

- **What it is:** A free library of source texts that are public domain or freely licensed, with volunteers proofreading scans and text.
- **Good for:** People who like books, history, languages, careful proofreading, and old documents.
- **Safe first task:** Proofread one page of an existing text.
- **Start here:** https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Wikisource:About
- **Watch out for:** Copyright. Do not add a text unless you are sure it is public domain or freely licensed.

### Wiktionary

- **What it is:** A free dictionary with definitions, examples, translations, and pronunciation for words in many languages.
- **Good for:** Language lovers, translators, teachers, and people who enjoy precise word meanings.
- **Safe first task:** Add one missing word or improve one definition with an example sentence and a source.
- **Start here:** https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Welcome,_newcomers
- **Watch out for:** Formatting rules differ by language. Check an existing entry before creating a new one.

### Wikibooks

- **What it is:** A free library of textbooks, manuals, and other educational books that anyone can edit.
- **Good for:** People who can explain a subject step by step, make diagrams, or organize long content.
- **Safe first task:** Fix one broken page or add one short, sourced section to an existing book.
- **Start here:** https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Wikibooks:Welcome
- **Watch out for:** Books need a clear scope and consistent structure. Check the project's naming and formatting rules.

### Wikinews

- **What it is:** A free news project written and reviewed by volunteers. Articles go through a review process before publication.
- **Good for:** People who like reporting, interviewing, fact-checking, and current events.
- **Safe first task:** Improve one existing article with a source, or pitch a story in the newsroom before writing it.
- **Start here:** https://en.wikinews.org/wiki/Wikinews:Welcome
- **Watch out for:** The review process, neutral tone, and source requirements. News becomes stale quickly.

### Wikiversity

- **What it is:** A free learning community with courses, lessons, and research projects created by volunteers.
- **Good for:** Teachers, students, trainers, and people who like making learning materials.
- **Safe first task:** Add one short, sourced learning resource or fix one broken lesson page.
- **Start here:** https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/Wikiversity:Welcome
- **Watch out for:** Quality varies. Check the project's welcome page and avoid uploading personal student data.

### Wikivoyage

- **What it is:** A free travel guide with practical information about destinations, written by travellers.
- **Good for:** Travellers, local experts, map makers, and photographers.
- **Safe first task:** Improve one destination page with a practical, up-to-date detail and a source.
- **Start here:** https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Wikivoyage:Welcome,_newcomers
- **Watch out for:** No advertising, no copied travel text, and no unsafe personal details about real people.

## Technical and coordination projects

### MediaWiki

- **What it is:** The open-source software that runs Wikipedia and many other wikis. Volunteers contribute code, documentation, testing, design, and translations.
- **Good for:** Developers, designers, technical writers, testers, and translators.
- **Safe first task:** Pick a good-first-task from the contribution page, set up the listed tools, and make one small documentation or code fix.
- **Start here:** https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/How_to_contribute
- **Watch out for:** Set up the development environment before claiming a task. Ask in the listed chat if you get stuck.

### Translatewiki.net

- **What it is:** A separate translation platform used to translate the interface of MediaWiki and other free software. It is not the same as translating encyclopedia articles.
- **Good for:** Bilingual people who want to make software easier to use in their language.
- **Safe first task:** Read the introduction, choose your language, and translate a small set of interface messages.
- **Start here:** https://translatewiki.net/wiki/Translating:Intro
- **Watch out for:** This is a separate community with its own rules. Follow its style guide for your language.

### Meta-Wiki

- **What it is:** A coordination wiki for the Wikimedia movement, including global policies, grants, campaigns, and discussions.
- **Good for:** People who like organizing, governance, outreach, and cross-project work.
- **Safe first task:** Read a current discussion and leave one clear, respectful comment, or help improve a campaign page.
- **Start here:** https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_projects
- **Watch out for:** Discussions can be long. Read the context before commenting, and stay civil.

### Other projects worth knowing

- **Wikifunctions:** A wiki of reusable functions. Good for coders, testers, and people who like small, well-defined tasks. Start here: https://www.wikifunctions.org/wiki/Wikifunctions:Introduction
- **Wikimedia Incubator:** A test space for new language editions of Wikimedia projects. Good for speakers of languages that do not yet have an edition. Start here: https://incubator.wikimedia.org/wiki/Incubator:Main_Page
- **Wikimedia Outreach:** A home for campaigns, teaching materials, and community outreach. Good for teachers, organizers, and event volunteers. Start here: https://outreach.wikimedia.org/wiki/Main_Page

## How to choose

Ask these five questions:

1. Do you want to write, collect media, organize data, translate, teach, or code?
2. Do you want to work alone or with a team?
3. Do you want to make one small fix or join a longer project?
4. Which languages do you speak?
5. How much time do you have today: 15 minutes, one hour, or a weekend?

The best first project is usually the one that matches something you already enjoy, not the one with the biggest audience.

## Important

- Wikimedia projects are run by volunteers. Be patient and assume good faith.
- Local rules matter. A rule you know from one project may not apply to another.
- No project allows harassment, privacy violations, or deliberate misinformation.
- If you are paid to edit or have a conflict of interest, disclose it and follow the local policy.
