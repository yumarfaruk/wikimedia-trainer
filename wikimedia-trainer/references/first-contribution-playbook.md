# First Contribution Playbook

Use this when someone is ready to make a real edit. The goal is one small, sourced, easy-to-review change.

## Before you start

1. **Create a free account.** Do not share it. A named account makes it easier to talk with other editors.
2. **Pick your language edition.** Policies and help pages can differ between languages.
3. **Read the project's start page.** It will tell you what the project accepts and where to get help.
4. **Find a small task.** Good options: fix a typo, add a missing link, add one source, proofread one page, or add one sourced fact.
5. **Use the sandbox if the project has one.** On Wikipedia, you can practice in your user sandbox before editing a live page.
6. **Keep the first edit small.** One clear improvement is better than ten risky changes.

## A safe Wikipedia edit, step by step

1. Open an article in your language and read the section you want to improve.
2. Click **Edit** (or **Edit source** if you need wikitext).
3. Make one small change. Do not rewrite the whole page.
4. Add a **reliable source** if you add a fact. A newspaper, book, journal, or official report is usually better than a blog or social post.
5. Write a short **edit summary**, such as `Fixed date per cited source` or `Added citation for population figure`.
6. Click **Show preview** and check that the page still looks right.
7. Click **Publish changes**.
8. Open the article again and check that your change is there.
9. If someone reverts it, go to the article's **talk page** and ask why. Do not keep changing it back.

## A safe Wikimedia Commons upload

1. Read **Commons:First steps** and **Commons:Project scope**.
2. Upload only a photo, diagram, or recording that you made yourself, or a file with a clear free license.
3. Use a clear title and description. Add the date and the place or subject.
4. Choose the correct license. If you do not understand the license, stop and ask for help.
5. Add useful categories so others can find the file.
6. For photos of identifiable people, check the consent and privacy rules before uploading.
7. Do not copy images from Google, social media, news sites, or other websites unless the license clearly allows it.

## A safe Wikidata edit

1. Read **Wikidata:Introduction**.
2. Find an item about a topic you know.
3. Add one missing statement with a reliable source.
4. Write a short edit summary.
5. Check that you did not remove or change existing sourced statements by mistake.
6. Do not use bots or mass-edit tools until you know the local rules and have agreement.

## Rules that matter everywhere

- **Neutral point of view:** Describe facts; do not advertise or campaign.
- **Reliable sources:** Facts need support. A source should be independent and trustworthy.
- **Copyright:** Write in your own words. Do not paste text or upload files you do not have the right to share.
- **Privacy:** Do not add private information about yourself or anyone else.
- **Civility:** Discuss the edit, not the person. Assume good faith.
- **Conflict of interest:** Disclose paid editing or a close personal connection, and follow the local policy.
- **Consensus:** For large or disputed changes, discuss first. Do not edit war.
- **No secrets:** Never put passwords, API keys, private addresses, or personal data into an edit or a file.

## Good and bad examples

| Bad first edit | Better first edit |
| --- | --- |
| Changes a date with no source and no summary. | Changes the date and adds a citation, with the summary `Corrected date per cited source`. |
| Copies a paragraph from a news website. | Summarizes the source in their own words and cites it. |
| Uploads a random image found on Google. | Uploads their own photo with a license, description, date, and categories. |
| Adds a long unsourced paragraph about a friend. | Adds one sourced fact about a notable topic. |
| Asks an agent to mass-edit 500 pages. | Starts with one page, learns the rules, and asks the community before any batch edit. |
| Argues after a reversion. | Asks on the talk page what was wrong and tries a smaller fix. |

## When you get stuck

- **Wikipedia:** Visit the Teahouse at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Teahouse
- **Wikimedia Commons:** Visit https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Help_desk
- **Wikidata:** Visit https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Project_chat
- **MediaWiki software:** Visit https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Project:Support_desk
- **General project list:** Visit https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_projects

Say what you tried, what happened, and what you expected. That makes it easy for someone to help.

## A simple message to ask for help

> Hello. I am new and I want to make a small, useful edit about [topic]. I read [help page] and I plan to [one specific change] using [source]. Is this the right place, and is there a cleanup list I should use? Thank you.

## A simple edit summary

> Added citation for [fact]. Source: [short source name].

Keep it short and specific. The edit summary helps other people understand your change.
